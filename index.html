How to read & use the contour ▾

Click to seek · drag to pan · ⌘/Ctrl+scroll, pinch or ± to zoom · drag a dot up or down to correct it · click the ruler at the top to seek. The line is the melody: indigo plateaus are notes to hold, while saffron connectors use three shapes: a vertical step, a compact hook for a quick bend, or a broad curve for meend. Each dot is a note the voice hits, named with its swara; a hollow dot is one to check by ear (sung between two swaras, or an inferred ornament). The faint grey line is the sung pitch itself (Sung toggles it). Solid grid lines are the song’s scale.

Note names: S = Sa; r = komal Re (1 semitone above Sa); R = shuddh Re (2); g = komal Ga (3); G = shuddh Ga (4); m = shuddh Ma (5); M = tivra Ma (6); P = Pa (7); d = komal Dha (8); D = shuddh Dha (9); n = komal Ni (10); N = shuddh Ni (11). A leading dot means the lower octave: .n is 2 semitones below Sa; .N is 1 below. So .n–R spans 4 semitones, while .N–r spans 2.

    Raag analysis

    The swaras this recording uses, sized by how much they’re sung. Tap one to highlight every place it appears. Thaat is the scale family; raag suggestions are a shortlist to check by ear, not a verdict. Intonation shows how each komal/teevra swara is actually pitched.

    Every sung syllable starts a fresh note (so repeated words on one pitch don't blur into one). Clean keeps the singable notes and the ornaments that touch them — murkis as (GRG)m, meends as P⌒S' — while dropping blips, stray pitches and vibrato wobble. Detailed shows every nuance; Simple is just the main notes. Raise strictness if background instruments leak in; lower it if quiet phrases go missing.

    Audio is processed entirely on this device — nothing is uploaded, no analytics, no third-party assets. Pitch (beta) transposes the recording to your own Sa (notation is unchanged — sargam is the same in any key); Speed slows it down without changing pitch.
